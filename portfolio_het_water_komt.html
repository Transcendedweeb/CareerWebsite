Het Water Komt - VR

Stakeholders: NTR

Date Finished:April 2024

Languages:Unity/C#

GitHub: Het Water Komt repo

Overview

The NTR VR Podwalks project was developed in collaboration with the Dutch public broadcaster NTR to bring their well-known Podwalk experiences into Virtual Reality. Traditionally, a Podwalk combines podcasts and location-based storytelling: listeners follow a walking route outdoors while listening to narratives tied to specific places.
With this project, we created a VR application prototype that allows users to experience these Podwalks from home, using a Meta Quest 2 headset. By combining 360° video, immersive audio, interactive overlays, and environmental effects, the application recreates the feeling of walking the route — without requiring physical presence or favorable weather conditions. This makes the Podwalk experience more accessible to people with physical limitations and allows anyone to enjoy the stories regardless of location.

Features

- 360° Podwalk Video Integration: The VR experience is built around high-resolution 360° video recordings of the Podwalk routes, projected onto a skybox for full immersion.
- Interactive Points of Interest: At specific locations, the video pauses and provides overlays with historical photos, background information, or additional narrative context. Users can choose whether to interact or continue walking.
- Immersive Environmental Effects: To enhance realism, effects such as rain, mist, and water simulations were integrated into the environment, connecting the story with the atmosphere.
- Contextual Overlays & Archival Media: Archival photos of events (such as floods) were aligned with real-world locations and superimposed on the video, showing the contrast between past and present.
- Smooth Transitions & Panoramas: For deeper storytelling, transitions between 360° video and modified panorama images (e.g., with adjusted water levels) were added, maintaining immersion without breaking perspective.

Challenges & Learnings

One of the main challenges we faced was working with the original 5K 360° video recordings. These files were extremely large (over 3GB) and could not be streamed without significant quality loss. To overcome this, we integrated the video files offline into the application. This solution preserved resolution and framerate but required careful optimization to avoid performance issues on the Meta Quest 2.
Another difficulty came from the hardware limitations of the VR headset itself. Since the project was developed exclusively for the Meta Quest 2, we had to ensure that the application ran smoothly within its technical constraints. This meant paying close attention to memory usage, frame stability, and post-processing effects. In the process, we learned how to balance visual fidelity with system performance to maintain immersion without overwhelming the hardware.
We also discovered the delicate balance between immersion and usability. For example, raycast pointers made interaction easier but visually disrupted the experience. Our solution was to let users decide when to enable or disable them.
Finally, accessibility remained a key consideration throughout the project. Since the core goal was to make Podwalks available to people who cannot physically walk the routes, we had to design interactions that were simple, comfortable, and intuitive for a wide audience. Working in short SCRUM sprints allowed us to test early, gather feedback, and adapt quickly. This iterative approach helped us deliver a functional prototype while also giving us valuable experience in professional project workflows.
